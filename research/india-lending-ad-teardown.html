Creative intelligenceIndia · September 2026

Nobody advertises the loan

Across 50-plus Indian lenders, almost none run a brand campaign about personal loans. Banks advertise deposits and fraud awareness. NBFCs advertise gold loans. The apps advertise speed, and one of them spent enough to buy 315 million views on a film that never mentions credit. Here is what each player is actually selling, and the three films worth your time.

01 — If you watch nothing else

Three films, three different arguments

Picked for what they teach about selling credit in India, not for craft alone. Each is verified live and carries its real view count.

01 / THE MONEY

Navi — “Hurrypur Auto Garage”

315,528,884 views4 months agoAgency: Sideways

An autorickshaw breaks down and the repair becomes an F1 pit stop. Navi built a fictional town, Hurrypur, where everything is absurdly fast — a proprietary world that generates infinite films about one idea without repeating a joke.

Why it matters: this is the biggest media buy in Indian fintech by an order of magnitude, and it is for the UPI app, not the loan book. Navi advertises payments to acquire users, then sells credit to them silently. Its loan creative is separate, functional, and unbranded.

02 / THE STRATEGY

Kissht — “When Sachin gets the hiccups”

767,566 viewsAug 2026Sachin Tendulkar

Every time Kissht approves a loan, Sachin Tendulkar gets hiccups — because so many people are thanking him at once. A joke about the brand’s own approval speed.

Why it matters: in February 2023 Kissht was blocked by MeitY in a sweep of apps with alleged Chinese links. It is Indian-owned and the block was revoked. Read the Tendulkar deal — he is ambassador and investor — as reputation repair: buy the most trusted man in India, then refuse solemnity. Post-crackdown lenders will copy this.

Watch →
03 / THE INSIGHT

Home Credit — “#KhudParNirbhar”

3,613,798 viewsAug 2026No celebrity

Ten ordinary protagonists, direct to camera, opening on a 24-year-old woman in Kanpur buying her first scooter. Financial independence mapped onto national independence.

Why it matters: Home Credit is the only lender arguing that borrowing is self-reliance rather than dependence. Its companion campaign, #LoanMilega, stages the family as a courtroom — “Ghar Adalat” — because it has correctly identified that the binding constraint on Indian household borrowing is social, not financial. The obstacle is the uncle at the table, not the interest rate.

Watch →

The fourth, if you have time: Muthoot FinCorp’s “SRK Brings Drama. We Bring the Gold Loan” (459,577 views). A businessman recruits Shah Rukh Khan to charm a loan manager — but the approval SMS lands before he can perform. It hires the ultimate symbol of influence in order to make him redundant. The argument is that you don’t need to be somebody to get money, which is a real dignity proposition. Note the trick, though: gold loans are secured, so the lender can afford to be gracious. Piramal makes the same argument for unsecured lending, which is far harder.

The Fibe anomaly. Fibe’s “Jiyo Full Size” is arguably the best creative idea in the category — aspirations literally shrink when money is short, a wedding horse shrinking mid-ceremony, resolved by flipping India’s dread-phrase “Paisa kahan se aayega?” into “Paisa yahan se aayega.” It is AI-generated, and it has 11 views. The sibling cuts have 14 and 26. A genuinely good idea with no media behind it is not a campaign; it is a portfolio piece.

The HDFC anomaly. India’s largest private bank promises ₹40 lakh in 10 seconds with zero documentation — to 16,174 people. Meanwhile its flagship brand investment is Vigil Aunty, a seven-episode comedy series on JioHotstar whose entire message is that fraudsters manufacture urgency, so slow down. The same bank tells you to hurry and to wait. That contradiction is the single most revealing thing in this dataset.

03 — Message territory

Who owns which claim

Two things nobody does: nobody leads on a low interest rate, and nobody says “no CIBIL” out loud. That message has migrated entirely into emotional proxies — almost certainly because of regulatory sensitivity.

CONTESTED — 3 BRANDSSpeed

Navi (Hurrypur), Kissht (5-minute approval), True Balance (“fatafat”). Three brands fighting over one undifferentiated claim.

HOME CREDITFamily permission

Uniquely owned. The Ghar Adalat courtroom and #KhudParNirbhar treat borrowing as a social negotiation, not a financial one.

mPOKKETAnti-gatekeeping

“Life shouldn’t be one long audition.” Naveen Polishetty’s real history of 1,000+ rejected auditions is the credit-policy argument. The tightest ambassador logic in the category.

OLYVRejection anxiety

“Salary achi hai… phir bhi loan reject?” Recruits people before they are loan-eligible. The most under-exploited territory in Indian lending.

FIBE / MONEYVIEWAnti-compromise

Don’t shrink your life. The strongest emotional territory — and Fibe currently has no media behind it.

CREDStatus & absurdity

Copied by everyone, owned by one. Rahul Dravid’s “Indiranagar ka gunda” hit 4M views in four days and lifted sign-ups 6–7x. It is the origin text for the whole category’s tone.

SNAPMINTPrice mechanic

0% EMI without a credit card. No brand layer at all — its most considered video is a merchant case study, because it is a B2B2C distribution business in a consumer costume.

NOW TABLE STAKESTrust & anti-fraud

KreditBee (Jaagte Raho), Moneyview, Home Credit, Stashfin, Olyv all run scam-awareness series. Post-crackdown, trust is a category defence, not a differentiator.

PIRAMALHow we underwrite

“Hum kaagaz se zyaada, neeyat dekhte hain” — we look at intent more than paperwork. The only major lender whose promise is about its credit policy rather than its speed.

The structural shift of 2026: brand films are being displaced by always-on content marketing. Moneyview has run no new celebrity film since the Ranbir Kapoor campaign — its channel is now money-mule scam explainers and credit-on-UPI education. mPokket runs four named formats including a scripted web series about someone ₹50,000 short on a deadline. These lenders are behaving like studios, not advertisers. Partly this is the RBI and MeitY effect: the IT ministry told platforms to stop carrying fraudulent loan-app ads, and Google has taken enforcement action on roughly 3,500 loan apps on Play Store in India.

Watch for the regulatory collision. RBI has issued draft Amendment Directions on the advertising, marketing and sale of financial products, covering dark patterns, compulsory bundling and incentive-driven mis-selling — and extending accountability to third-party agents, affiliates and influencers. Bajaj’s Loan Utsav, which bundles OTT subscriptions and Zomato Gold with loan disbursal, is precisely the structure that framework targets. Reported effective dates vary across sources; verify against the RBI circular before relying on one.

The pattern underneath: the biggest personal-loan distributors in India — Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, DMI — do no loan advertising whatsoever, because the offer travels inside someone else’s interface. If you want to know where personal-loan marketing is actually going in India, it is not into films. It is into pre-approved offers surfaced at the moment of payment.

07 — Method

How this was built, and what it can’t tell you

The Meta Ad Library could not be read. Three routes were tried and all failed: the ad-library scraper returned HTTP 402 — upstream account out of credits; Facebook served a bot challenge (403) to both direct requests and a real browser; and the Graph API ads_archive endpoint requires an access token. So this is not an ad-library readout — there are no impression bands, spend ranges, creative-variant counts or run dates here.

What replaced it. Campaign facts come from Indian ad trade press. Video discovery and view counts come from scraping YouTube search metadata directly. Every video link was then verified live via YouTube’s oEmbed endpoint, returning the real title and owning channel — which caught several confidently-cited films that do not exist.

Treat view counts as a spend proxy, not engagement. Lender ad films accumulate views overwhelmingly through paid in-stream placement. A 315M-view film means a very large media buy, not 315 million people who chose to watch. Counts were read in September 2026 and move continuously.

Known gaps. A handful of films could not be verified to a watch URL — IndusInd’s “Account Bada Toh Dil Bada”, RBL’s Gukesh “Right Moves” series, and Hero FinCorp’s specific “Nariyal Phodo, Sar Kyun?” cut, though its sibling films are here. CRED’s films circulate mainly via press embeds rather than stable YouTube IDs. Two mPokket uploads with ~30M views resolve in search but block embedding, so they are cited but not linked.

One dating correction worth carrying forward. Several campaigns widely described as current are not: Poonawalla’s “Log toh Sawaal Karenge Hi” is September 2023, Axis’s “Dil Se Open — Aapke Liye” is 2022, and AU’s Kiara Advani “Soch Badlo” is October 2023 (the December 2025 relaunch uses different faces).
