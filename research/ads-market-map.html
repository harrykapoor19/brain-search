The competitive reality

Everyone here is building on someone else's land

The independent ad-tech market is not competing with itself. It is competing with the free automation shipped by the platforms whose inventory it resells. Advantage+ alone, at a $75B run rate, is roughly ten times larger than the entire independent software market shown below — and it is a feature, not a company.

The five positions that survive

Ground the platforms structurally cannot or will not take:

  • Cross-platform. Meta will never optimize your TikTok budget against your Meta budget.
  • Creative volume and originality. Platforms remix your assets. They do not invent concepts, actors or campaigns.
  • Independent measurement. A platform grading its own homework is a permanent conflict of interest.
  • Workflow, governance and scale. Bulk launch, QA, approvals, multi-account agency operations.
  • Embedded and vertical distribution. Merchants inside Shopify or WooCommerce, and franchise networks, that platform self-serve funnels never reach.

The verdict

Which founders I'd bet on

My filter is one question. When Meta or Google ships your feature for free, do you still have a business? Distribution, brand and even revenue are weak evidence. Prior experience of being run over by a platform — and a deliberate choice of ground the platform will not take — are strong evidence.

Tier 1 — I would write the cheque

4 founders

Yuchen Wu

Co-founder & CEO, MAIBest founder–market fit

Ten years, 2010–2020, as an engineering director at Google building the advertising system. Then VP of Engineering at Instacart. Tsinghua bachelor's, Northwestern PhD. Co-founder Jian Wang is also ex-Google and ex-Instacart. Founded December 2024; $25M seed led by Kleiner Perkins by October 2025; already managing millions of dollars of Google Ads spend monthly.

Why I'd bet: this is the single best founder–market fit in the dataset. He spent a decade building the machine everyone else is trying to out-guess from the outside. He knows which parts of the auction are opaque, which levers still matter after automation, and where the residual alpha lives. When an advertiser asks what he can do that Performance Max can't, he is one of a handful of people who can answer from first principles.

Risk: SMB is where platform automation lands hardest and cheapest, and SMB churn is brutal. He is fishing in the most contested pond.

Joshua Xu

Co-founder & CEO, HeyGenBest fundamentals

CMU master's in CS, former lead engineer at Snapchat. $200M ARR as of June 2026, doubled in eight months, having burned roughly $25M of $74M raised — about $2.70 of ARR per dollar of equity, better than Zoom or Datadog at IPO.

Why I'd bet: these are the best fundamentals in the market and it isn't close. Growing at that rate while cash-flow break-even means the product sells itself and the unit economics work without a subsidy. HeyGen also sits on the right side of the platform question — Meta will remix your existing assets, but it will not give you a consistent synthetic spokesperson in forty languages.

Risk: video model quality is converging fast and the frontier labs are a real threat. Defensibility has to come from workflow and likeness rights, not raw generation.

Alex Mashrabov

Founder & CEO, Higgsfield AIRight person, hard price

His mother designed rockets for the Soviet space program. He started programming at 10 and was ranked top-3 globally in competitive programming by 20. He co-founded AI Factory, acquired by Snap for $166M in 2020, whose technology became the backbone of Snapchat's face filters and Cameos — then spent three years as Snap's Director of Generative AI shipping MyAI to hundreds of millions of users. Left in September 2023 to found Higgsfield.

Why I'd bet on the person: an exit to a platform, then running generative AI inside that platform at consumer scale, is close to perfect preparation for building consumer-scale generative video. The results follow: ~$700M annualized revenue, 30M users, and a valuation path of $1.0B → $1.3B → $5.4B in under a year.

The catch is the price. At $5.4B on roughly $700M of consumption revenue you are underwriting continued hypergrowth in a category where switching costs are low and credit-based revenue can decay as fast as it compounds. I'd back Mashrabov without hesitation; I'd think hard about this entry.

Reza Khadjavi

Co-founder & CEO, MotionBest risk-adjusted

The most instructive story in the market. He co-founded Shoelace in 2015, one of the first apps automating Facebook advertising for Shopify merchants. In 2018 Facebook and Shopify deepened their direct integration and erased the gap Shoelace existed to bridge. In his words: "The writing was on the wall that the market didn't really need a product like ours anymore." By 2020 gross monthly revenue churn hit 9%, nearly double the SaaS average. He chose to pivot rather than die — "it eventually came down to pivoting to an agency or death… I decided: let's choose to live" — and rebuilt the company into Motion.

Why I'd bet: he has already been killed once by exactly the force that threatens every company on this page, and he survived it and learned the lesson. Motion is deliberately positioned where the platforms have no incentive to go: cross-platform creative analytics for the human creative strategist. Roughly $50M ARR as of August 2025, from single-digit millions to eight figures within three months of launching AI Employees, on only $42M raised. That is a founder who chose his ground with scar tissue.

Tier 2 — strong, would look hard

  • Ikkjin Ahn — Moloco. Early ML engineer on YouTube. Built ~$400M of revenue as the ranking engine for everyone who isn't Meta or Google. Superb pedigree; late-stage entry at $1.5–2.0B.
  • Melissa Burdick — Pacvue. Ten years at Amazon, then bootstrapped to a nine-figure exit with zero VC. The purest capital-efficiency record here and a genuine category definer in retail media.
  • Romain Torres & Dylan Fournier — Arcads. ~$15M ARR, profitable, reportedly about 8 people, on a $16M seed. If those figures hold, the best hidden risk-adjusted bet in the set.
  • Eric Mayhew & Mike Sullivan — Fluency. Profitable since 2020, bootstrapped to $3B in media spend and 250,000 campaigns a month before taking a single round. Unglamorous ad-ops infrastructure, which is exactly why platforms won't touch it.
  • Vitaly Pecherskiy — StackAdapt. Stepped up from COO to CEO in 2024; now runs a $500M+ revenue business at a $2.5B valuation.
  • Gil Allouche — Metadata.io. Ex-VP Marketing at Qubole who built the tool for his own job. B2B is the segment least exposed to Meta's automation.
  • Frederick Vallaeys — Optmyzr. Among Google's first ~500 employees, a decade on AdWords; bootstrapped to $15–24M with $5.3B in spend managed. Won't be a venture outcome; will still be here in ten years.

Tier 3 — I would pass

  • Thin wrappers on Advantage+ or Performance Max. Most sub-$5M-ARR SMB self-serve tools. Their roadmap is written by Meta's product team.
  • Companies whose raise vastly exceeds revenue. VidMob: $208.9M raised against ~$18.3M estimated ARR. The cap table is now the problem.
  • Single-channel rules engines. Precisely what Marin Software was, and it liquidated for $5.5M.
  • "Autonomous AI media buyer" with no platform-insider pedigree. See below.

If I could make only one bet: MAI at seed, Motion at growth. MAI has the best founder–market fit and the highest ceiling if agentic buying works at all. Motion is the best risk-adjusted position — a founder who already survived platform absorption, deliberately building on ground Meta has no reason to take, at ~$50M ARR on $42M raised.

Best pure business today, if you can get in: HeyGen. Nothing else combines $200M ARR, doubling in eight months, and cash-flow break-even.

Due diligence · read before backing any agentic ad startup

The graveyard

Every pre-LLM "autonomous AI media buyer" is dead. This is the pattern the current cohort has to explain away.

Icon Collapsed, then inverted

Founded 2024 by Kennan Davison (previously founder of Skio). Backed by Founders Fund with a $9.2M seed plus executives from OpenAI, Ramp, Flexport, Cognition and Pika. Paid $12M for the icon.com domain in April 2025. Claimed $5M ARR and 76 staff.

The autonomous "AI CMO" never worked reliably — users reported poor AI voice quality, a clunky interface and billing problems. The team vanished from LinkedIn by February 2026 and reporters declared it dead in March 2026. The site is now back online selling human-made UGC ads — "38 Human UGC ads (100% real / not AI)" — a complete inversion of the founding thesis. Note the shutdown was inferred by journalists; the company never announced one.

Albert.ai Asset sale, Mar 2022

The best-known autonomous media buyer of its generation. Assets sold to Zoomd in a cash-and-stock transaction.

Adext AI Ceased operations 2024

Raised at a $20M valuation in 2016 promising fully automated audience testing and budget reallocation. Its LinkedIn page now states the company ceased operations in 2024.

The lesson is not that agentic ad buying can't work. It is that the wrapper-around-an-API version doesn't, and that "we'll automate the media buyer" is a claim this market has heard and punished three times. Which is exactly why a decade inside Google's ad system matters more than a pitch deck.

Method

How to read these numbers

  • Private-company revenue is largely modelled. Figures marked "est." come from Latka, Tracxn or Growjo and are frequently wrong by a wide margin, especially with usage-based pricing. Unmarked figures come from company announcements, SEC filings or credible press.
  • Valuations are last-round post-money unless noted — they reflect the date of the round, not today.
  • Two source conflicts worth flagging: a PitchBook-derived snippet claimed Proxima raised an $80M seed in January 2026, but the verifiable round is a $12M Series A from April 2024 led by Mucker Capital. Madgicx is listed as bootstrapped by Latka and Crunchbase while a Tracxn/PitchBook snippet names PeakSpan Capital as an investor.
  • Meta and LinkedIn ad-library links are keyword searches and will return other advertisers mentioning the same term; Google links are advertiser-domain lookups and are exact. An empty result usually means the company isn't currently advertising on that platform.
  • LinkedIn blocks automated profile fetching, so founder profile links in the full report are sometimes search links rather than direct profiles.
