Market research India · September 2026

India’s unsecured lending market is two markets

A ₹16.5 lakh crore personal-loan book, growing 12.9% a year. Banks hold 73% of the value. Fintech apps hold 45% of the accounts and originate 77% of every loan written. There is no single market share here — there is a choice of which market you are in.

01 — The structural fact

One book, two businesses

The same ₹16.5 lakh crore looks completely different depending on whether you count rupees or borrowers. A bank writes 1.41 crore loans a year at an average of ₹4.91 lakh. A digital NBFC writes 13.23 crore loans at an average of ₹16,238. They barely compete.

Counted in rupees
73%
Banks

₹12.04 lakh cr outstanding across 3.82 cr accounts. Average balance ₹3.15 lakh. 76% of bank sanction value goes to tickets above ₹5 lakh — and 0% below ₹50,000.

Counted in borrowers
45%
Digital NBFCs

₹1.43 lakh cr across 5.50 cr accounts — 9% of value. Average balance ₹26,053. 43% of their sanction value is in tickets under ₹50,000, where banks are absent.

The gap that explains everything else: 34% of digital-NBFC lending by value goes to high or very-high credit-risk borrowers, against 11% for banks. Fintechs did not win the small-ticket market on technology alone — they won it by underwriting people the banks decline.

The market is barbelling. Value from tickets above ₹10 lakh went 29.2% → 32.8% → 35.7% across three years, while sub-₹1-lakh loans held 88.6% of all volume. Banks are moving up-ticket and fintechs are holding the floor. The ₹1–5 lakh middle is the thinnest part of the market.

06 — Vertical lenders

Where the segment is the moat

Vertical lenders underwrite something the bureau cannot see: a college enrolment, a Swiggy earnings history, a hospital bill, a distributor ledger. Each segment below has a leader, and most leaders are still under ₹5,000 crore.

18–28, college students and first-jobbers, ₹0–25k/month, thin or no bureau file. ₹500–₹50,000, 7–90 days, very high APR.

Employment channel — the giant

Government, defence and PSU salary-account holders, plus pensioners. Underwritten on employer, not on borrower. The largest unsecured vertical in India by a wide margin.

  • SBI Xpress Credit₹3,75,348 cr
  • PSU bank schemesBoB, PNB, Canara
  • SBI pension loans₹2.5–14 lakh

White space. Four verticals have demand and no scaled lender: freelancers and creators (no player at all), senior citizens beyond bank pension schemes, rent — which today is card-payment rails at 1–1.75% fees, not lending — and healthcare BNPL, which has been open since ZestMoney shut in 2023. Doctors and professionals are served only by Bajaj and Poonawalla, both underwriting off a registration number.

The TG is bifurcating, not shrinking. Banks moved up — RBL to ₹40,000, Kotak requiring a degree, Federal requiring three years in the same job — while growth moved below them to Home Credit at ₹10,000 with housewives eligible, and to the DMI–Google Pay rail in tier-II and beyond. RBI’s own projection that fintechs reach 56.8% of small-ticket PL by March 2026 is the same fact from the other side.

09 — Regulation and risk

The rule that reshaped the market is still in force

RBI raised the risk weight on unsecured consumer credit from 100% to 125% in November 2023. The February 2025 rollback is widely misread: it restored microfinance and bank-to-NBFC weights only. The 125% on unsecured personal loans and cards was never reversed.

Do not read those improving ratios as improving underwriting. The denominator grew 28.9% in one year, which flatters every ratio in the table. Against that: RBI’s June 2026 FSR puts unsecured retail GNPA at 1.7% versus 0.7% secured; household debt is 45.5% of GDP; 15% of FY26 originations went to already over-leveraged borrowers, against 5% in FY17; and Redseer puts the digital-PL industry annualised loss rate at 9.35% in FY25, up from 7.80%. Vintages written into this growth will season through FY27.

11 — Confidence

What this doesn’t know

Not disclosed anywhere. ICICI Bank, Aditya Birla Capital, L&T Finance and Piramal do not break out an absolute personal-loan book — only growth rates. FACE publishes no member-level league table, so the fintech ranking here is inferred from AUM disclosures rather than reported share.

Series break to watch. FACE changed its peer group in the Dec-2025 edition to 110+ digital NBFCs and rebased to Apr-2022. FY25 sanctions therefore appear as both ₹1.07 lakh cr and ₹1.55 lakh cr depending on edition. Do not chain the two series — a growth rate computed across them is meaningless.

Reconciliation warning. RBI’s “other personal loans” (~₹15.4 lakh cr, banks only) and CRIF’s all-lender ₹14.6 lakh cr are not the same universe or the same product definition. Use CRIF and FACE for share, RBI for bank trend, and never mix them in one calculation.

Single-source figures. Fibe’s average ticket is published as both ₹77,987 and ₹55,158. mPokket’s FY25 revenue appears as both ₹2,157 cr and ₹356 cr on one source page. Auxilo’s AUM units are printed inconsistently. Download counts throughout are company-stated, not Play Store bands.
