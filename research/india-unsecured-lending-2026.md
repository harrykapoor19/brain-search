# India Unsecured Lending — Market Map, Share, Target Groups & Ad Libraries

**As of September 2026.** FY26 = year ended 31 Mar 2026. "PL" = unsecured personal loans (excludes credit cards, consumer-durable loans, gold, housing, vehicle unless stated). ⚠ marks a figure that is uncertain, single-sourced, or derived rather than reported.

---

## 1. The short version

- The unsecured PL book is **₹16.5 lakh cr (Mar-2026), +12.9% YoY**, across **12.24 cr active loans**. FY26 originations were **₹11.45 lakh cr, +28.9%** — a sharp re-acceleration after FY25's −2.2% contraction caused by RBI's risk-weight hike.
- **The market splits in two, and the split is the whole story.** Banks hold **73% of the value** but only 31% of accounts. Digital NBFCs (fintech apps) hold **9% of value but 45% of accounts**, and originate **77% of all loan volume** at an average ticket of **₹16,238** against banks' **₹4.91 lakh**.
- So there is no single "market share" number. On value it's an incumbent bank market. On volume it's a fintech market. Anyone entering picks which of those two games they're playing.
- **Horizontal apps have consolidated into ~6 winners** (Moneyview, Navi, KreditBee, Fibe, Kissht, InCred) all now at ₹6,000–20,000 cr AUM and all IPO-bound or listed. The mid-tail (CASHe, LoanTap, Upwards, Niro, Fi, Simpl) has shut, shrunk, or been acquired.
- **Verticals are where the unclaimed ground is.** Education, gig/EWA, healthcare, travel and merchant credit each have a leader under ₹5,000 cr, and several have no scaled player at all (freelancers/creators, senior citizens, rent).
- **The TG splits cleanly by ticket size**: banks serve 750+ CIBIL salaried at ₹5L+; horizontal apps serve ₹25k–75k/month salaried aged ~32 in tier-2/3 at ₹15k–80k tickets; verticals serve people the bureau can't score at all.

### 3d. Small-ticket (<₹50k) — the fintech stronghold

Fintech apps **56.8%**, NBFCs 30.7%, banks 10.1% of small-ticket PL; fintech loan growth +41.6% YoY (RBI FSR Jun-2026, [secondary source ⚠](https://www.barristery.in/2026/07/rbi-financial-stability-report-2026.html)). TransUnion CIBIL puts fintechs at **89% of industry STPL volumes**, with STPL = 92% of fintech originations ([CIBIL](https://newsroom.transunioncibil.com/indias-fintech-lending-expands-with-younger-rural-borrowers-driving-growth/)).

### 3e. Ticket-size distribution — CRIF, Q4 FY26 (all lenders)

| Ticket band | % of value | % of volume |
|---|---|---|
| < ₹1 lakh | 18.8% | **88.6%** |
| ₹1–2 lakh | 10.4% | 4.8% |

| ₹5–10 lakh | 18.7% | 1.8% |

Share of value from >₹10L tickets: 29.2% (Q4 FY24) → 32.8% → **35.7%** (Q4 FY26). The market is barbelling — banks going up-ticket, fintechs holding volume at the bottom.

## 8. Regulation & credit performance

| Date | Measure |
|---|---|
| **16 Nov 2023** | Risk weight on unsecured consumer credit **100% → 125%** for banks and NBFCs; cards to 150%/125%; +25pp on bank exposure to NBFCs |
| **25 Feb 2025** | **Partial rollback only** — microfinance back to 100%, bank-to-NBFC restored from 1 Apr 2025. **The 125% on unsecured PL and cards was NOT rolled back** and still stands |
| **8 May 2025** | RBI Digital Lending Directions 2025 — consolidates DLG + LSP outsourcing; direct fund flow RE↔borrower, KFS/APR mandatory, cooling-off, data localisation |
| **Jul 2025** | Public DLA Directory live; DLA reporting to CIMS |
| **1 Nov 2025** | Multi-lender LSP disclosure framework in force |
| **2026** | No further PL risk-weight relief found. Treat any claim of a 2026 cut as unverified ⚠ |

**Growth impact:** originations went +17.1% (FY24) → **−2.2% (FY25)** → **+28.9% (FY26)**. Digital NBFC value growth 19% → 39%; banks −8% → +24%.

**Delinquency — improving at the top, still hot at the bottom:**

| Metric | Mar-2025 | Mar-2026 |
|---|---|---|
| All-lender PAR 31–90 | 2.0% | **1.6%** |
| All-lender PAR 91–180 | 1.1% | 0.8% |
| PAR 91–180, <₹1L tickets | 3.32% | **2.27%** |
| PAR 91–180, >₹10L tickets | — | 0.33% |
| Digital-NBFC 90+ DPD | 2.7% | **1.4%** |
| PSU bank PAR 31–90 | 2.61% | 2.14% |
| Private bank PAR 31–90 | 1.11% | 0.78% |

But: RBI FSR Jun-2026 puts **unsecured retail GNPA at 1.7% vs 0.7% secured**; household debt is 45.5% of GDP; **15% of FY26 originations went to already over-leveraged borrowers, against 5% in FY17**; and Redseer puts the digital-PL industry annualised loss rate at **9.35% (FY25), up from 7.80%**. The bureau numbers look better because the denominator grew 29%, not because underwriting got safer.

**Casualties 2023–2026:** ZestMoney (shut), Fi Money consumer (shut Mar-26), Simpl (RBI halt Sep-25), Niro (shut Oct-25), CASHe (90% AUM collapse), Kinara (rescue), Lendingkart (control sale), Eduvanz/Rupifi/SanKash (revenue collapse), Hero FinCorp (unsecured halted), IIFL (PL discontinued). Acquired: axio→Amazon, IndiaLends→Freo, Jify→Moneyview, Home Credit→TVS, Avail→Ola, Bueno→BetterPlace.

### Bank & NBFC marketing

Almost no lender runs a *personal-loan-specific* brand campaign. Celebrity spend goes to the masterbrand (Axis/Deepika Padukone, IDFC FIRST/Amitabh Bachchan, Shriram/Rahul Dravid, AU/Aamir Khan + Kiara Advani, Kotak/Ranveer Singh, Tata Capital/Shubman Gill, SBI/MS Dhoni + Smriti Mandhana, Bank of Baroda/Sachin Tendulkar) or to gold loans (L&T/Jasprit Bumrah, Muthoot/Shah Rukh Khan). PL itself is sold through promo mechanics — Bajaj's "Loan Utsav 2026" reward bundle, IndusInd's 2% processing fee — and pre-approved in-app offers.

The two genuine PL-positioning campaigns sit at opposite ends of the same market: **Poonawalla's "Log toh Sawaal Karenge Hi"** pitches balance transfer at prime, high-CIBIL borrowers already paying high EMIs elsewhere; **Home Credit's #LoanMilega** pitches access to first-generation earners. Hero FinCorp runs "Nariyal Phodo, Sar Kyun?" with Delhi Capitals title sponsorship instead of a celebrity.

## 10. Data gaps and conflicts

**Gaps:** ICICI Bank / Aditya Birla Capital / L&T Finance / Piramal do not disclose absolute PL books. FACE publishes no member-level league table, so fintech ranking is inferred from AUM. Full-market <₹10k vs ₹10k–50k volume split is unpublished. Play Store download bands could not be read programmatically — download figures shown are company-stated.

**Conflicts flagged:** FACE changed its peer group in the Dec-2025 edition (110+ digital NBFCs, rebased to Apr-2022), so FY25 appears as both ₹1.07 lakh cr and ₹1.55 lakh cr sanctioned — **do not chain the two series**. Fibe's average ticket is reported as both ₹77,987 and ₹55,158. mPokket FY25 revenue appears as both ₹2,157 cr and ₹356 cr on the same source page. Auxilo's AUM units are printed inconsistently. CRIF's <₹1L PAR (2.27%) and FACE's digital 90+ DPD (1.4%) differ because of different windows and peer sets, not because one is wrong.
